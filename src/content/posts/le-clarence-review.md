[Le Clarence](https://www.le-clarence.paris/en/) is the prettiest restaurant I have eaten in. Not “pretty for a Michelin place.” The prettiest. You walk into a mansion off the Champs-Élysées, up a spiral staircase, and into rooms that feel like a French castle. I sat at a round table under an oval portrait, with red velvet chairs and candles, and I did feel a bit like a Disney princess. I am only slightly embarrassed to write that.

The food is the prestigious French version of this. A lot of courses. I got full. All of it was delicious. The service is high-end and precise, the kind of French room where everything is supposed to be perfect, but they were more relaxed than I expected. Nobody hovered. It did not feel like being looked after by bodyguards. It still felt expensive, in a good way.

## The rooms

The building is an 1884 hôtel particulier. The hall is checkerboard marble, a curved stair with a floral runner, a gold figure holding a candle, flowers in a tall vase. You go up, and the house keeps opening into different rooms. One is a library: books to the ceiling, plates set into the shelves, a marble fireplace, a bronze horse, red chairs, a green carpet with big flowers. Another is all wood paneling, a portrait of a woman in an oval frame, a small round table for two, candles lit before anyone sits down.

That second room is the one I mean. If you want to feel like you are in an old French story, book this and ask for a quiet table. The light is warm. It is not a dark, moody salon. It is bright enough to see the wood.

Chef Andrea Capasso has run the kitchen since September 2025. The house has held two Michelin stars since 2017, and the 2026 guide kept them under him. The wine list is enormous, and a lot of it is Domaine Clarence Dillon, which owns the restaurant. You are drinking from the same family as Haut-Brion.

## Le Clarence menu and what it costs

There is no single fixed menu. Lunch, Tuesday to Friday, is listed as three sequences for €150, four for €250, or six for €380. At dinner, four sequences are €280 and six are €430, before wine. They also do a shorter weekday lunch of starters, a main, and dessert if you do not want the long version. Prices are on [the restaurant page](https://www.le-clarence.paris/en/the-restaurant/). We did not count courses. Plates kept coming until I was actually full, which almost never happens to me at this kind of dinner, because the portions look small until the sixth one.

The phone is [+33 1 82 82 10 10](tel:+33182821010). They are open Tuesday through Saturday for lunch and dinner, and closed Sunday and Monday.

## What we actually ate

I do not have the printed name of every plate. This is what was in front of me.

Oysters on the half shell, three of them, each with a different garnish, on a gold tray with bread and a glass of champagne. Then three fried balls on a napkin, hot, with two little bowls of sauce. A clear red broth in a white cup, with tiny white flowers floating on it. A silver coupe of fruit, rhubarb-colored pieces and something yellow and glossy. Another silver coupe with a white base, a red slice, orange flowers, and a scatter of pollen or crushed nuts. An artichoke, braised until the leaves went dark, with a green herb salad and a brown sauce, and a half lemon on the table for someone else.

Fish came more than once. A fillet with a browned top on asparagus and cucumber, finished with a lot of dill. A second fish in a pale creamy sauce, the skin crisp and peppered, sitting over something soft. And a whole fish carved at the table from a big silver trolley, by a server in a navy suit, in the wood-paneled room. That is the French part. They still do the trolley. They just do not make a speech about it.

A glossy brown bun split and filled with pale sliced meat and a green sauce dusted with red spice. And a stack under a pale sauce that ran down the sides, finished with a snowfall of cheese. I would not swear to the name of that last one. It was rich, and it was good, and by then I was done.

## If I had to choose

If I had to pick one Michelin restaurant in Paris, I would send you to [Alliance](/blog/alliance-paris-review/). The food and the night were better there. It feels more like New York: modern, funny, the kitchen on the other side of a window.

Come to Le Clarence if you want the castle. Super French, classic, the staircase, the portrait, the feeling that you are in an old house and someone has already lit the candles. This is that place.

[Le Taillevent](/blog/le-taillevent-review/) is the moodier one. Darker light, more formal, less of this bright Parisian-castle look, and it is the famous address. You really cannot go wrong with any of the three. They are just different rooms.
