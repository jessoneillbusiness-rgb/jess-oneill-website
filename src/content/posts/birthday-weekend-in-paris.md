Paris for my birthday was the only plan I wanted. A long weekend, one beautiful hotel room, and nowhere I had to be before the coffee arrived.

I came for the clichés and stayed for the way the city slows you down: butter at breakfast, a walk with no destination, and dinner that feels like the reason you packed the good dress.

## Where I stayed in Paris

The room did half the celebrating for me. Dark floral wallpaper, a heavy red curtain, and a bottle of Abatilles on the table next to an “I love Paris” tee I fully intended to wear out.

## French breakfast in Paris

## A walk from Rue de Turbigo

After coffee I sat outside on Rue de Turbigo, iced latte in hand, the post office awning across the street and the city going past at café speed. This is the Paris I want when people ask what to do with an empty afternoon: one table, one drink, no agenda.

Then the walk. Haussmann stone, black iron balconies, a hard blue sky. I stopped in front of a limestone façade in a white skirt and let the building be the outfit. A little later I was in a boutique with a pink phone and a rack of clothes behind me, which is also a perfectly valid way to see Paris.

## Lunch with an Eiffel Tower view

The birthday lunch was outside, under white umbrellas, in the yellow and blue again. Hedge, sky, a terrace that felt like summer had made a reservation.

<figure class="post-figure">
  <img src="/images/posts/paris-birthday/paris-terrace-lunch-outfit.jpg" alt="Jess in a yellow top and blue lace on a sunny Paris restaurant terrace" />
  <figcaption>Terrace weather, birthday lunch</figcaption>
</figure>

The table faced the Eiffel Tower. Tuna with herbs, skinny fries, grilled fish, a bright salad, rosé, and a basket of bread we did not pretend to share politely. If you book one thing for a birthday weekend in Paris, book a table where you can see the tower without leaving your chair.

## How to plan a birthday weekend in Paris

A few things I would do again, and that I would tell a friend who searched “birthday weekend in Paris” and landed here:

- Stay somewhere with a window and a room you want to get ready in. The hotel is part of the trip.
- Give one morning entirely to a French breakfast. Baguette, butter, coffee, no schedule underneath it.
- Sit at a café before you sightsee. Rue de Turbigo was perfect because nothing about it was a monument.
- Book one lunch or dinner with an Eiffel Tower view, then stop chasing the tower for the rest of the weekend.
- Save one dinner for something celebratory. Caviar, escargot, a dress. Paris is good at occasions.
- Walk the Haussmann streets in late light. The balconies are the postcard.

> Paris on my birthday felt less like a city I visited and more like a table I got to sit at.

Until the next candle, Paris. You knew exactly what to do.
