Locarno sits where Switzerland starts speaking Italian. Lake Maggiore is at the bottom of the view, the mountains close the other three sides, and a weekend here moves at the speed of a long lunch.

I came for the water and stayed for the terraces. Every plan I made ended with a table, a glass, and the lake still in the corner of my eye.

## A terrace above Lake Maggiore

The first morning I understood the trip was the one I spent outside in a black polka-dot dress, white chairs around me, the town across the water and the peaks behind it. You do not need an itinerary when the view is doing this much work.

## Where I woke up

The hotel had a cedar sauna with a glass corner and a stone wall, which is a very Swiss way of recovering from a very Italian dinner. I used it more than once.

<figure class="post-figure">
  <img src="/images/posts/locarno/locarno-hotel-sauna.jpg" alt="A light wood and glass sauna in a Locarno hotel" />
  <figcaption>The sauna, which earned its place on the schedule</figcaption>
</figure>

And one balcony had a turquoise awning, two glasses, and a bottle in ice, with the harbor and the mountains framed like they had been hung there on purpose. If you book a room in Locarno, book the one that faces Lake Maggiore. The view is the amenity.

## Breakfast, then a walk

Breakfast was a pistachio croissant torn open, a cappuccino, and an iced coffee, taken slowly enough that the table became the plan. Ticino does this part perfectly: Swiss punctuality, Italian pastry.

After that I sat in a park on a red bench, clipped round trees in a row, a green mountain filling the sky. Locarno’s parks are part of the walk to the lake, not a detour from it.

## Lunch under the trees

One afternoon the table was outside, under lemon trees and a stone arch: breadsticks, cheese, prosciutto, salami, and torn bread. It felt like the start of a party that never needed to move indoors.

<figure class="post-figure">
  <img src="/images/posts/locarno/locarno-garden-antipasti.jpg" alt="Outdoor antipasti in Locarno with bread, cheese, cured meat, and lemon trees" />
  <figcaption>The garden table I would repeat</figcaption>

Then the copper pan arrived. Wide pasta in red sauce, torn mozzarella, basil, and enough olive oil to make the afternoon official. This is the food I mean when I tell someone to eat well in Locarno: Ticino cooking, eaten slowly, with the lake somewhere nearby.

## Dinner, still outside

Dinner stayed in the open air. A steak with a dark sauce, summer vegetables, white wine, and roses on the table. Then a quieter plate: pork with a green sauce, herbs, and a glass of white wine. I wore black lace and carried a small embroidered bag, which felt right for a town that dresses up without trying.

## How to spend a weekend in Locarno

If you are searching for what to do in Locarno, this is the version I would hand a friend:

- Stay somewhere with a terrace or balcony on Lake Maggiore. The town makes sense from above.
- Give one morning to breakfast and nothing else. A croissant and a cappuccino will take an hour if you let them.
- Walk the park and the harbor. The sailboats and the Swiss flags are the picture you came for.
- Eat outside. Ticino is Italian-speaking Switzerland, and the long lunch is the point.
- Leave one evening unplanned. The mountains turn blue and the tables stay out.
- Use the sauna if your hotel has one. You will earn it.

> Locarno is a lake town that feeds you like Italy and frames you like Switzerland.

I would go back for the view alone. The pasta would just be a very good excuse.
